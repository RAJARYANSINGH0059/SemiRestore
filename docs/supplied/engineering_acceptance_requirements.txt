UNDERSTAND THE NOVA-LOC PROBLEM

Validate the precise requirements from the supplied materials. The apparent core problem is wafer-inspection localization failure caused by multiple interacting sources of ambiguity:

PHYSICAL DRIFT: Thermal expansion, vibration, mechanical slack, or other displacement can move the actual target away from its expected coordinate.

SCALE MISMATCH: The reference template and target region may appear at different sizes, requiring scale-aware matching and potentially multi-scale searches.

IMAGE DEGRADATION: Noise, blur, contrast variation, missing edges, and degraded SEM imagery can weaken matching evidence.

REPETITIVE STRUCTURES: DRAM grids, FinFET gate patterns, and other repetitive layouts can create visually similar candidate regions, causing false matches.

COMPOUNDING AMBIGUITY: An expanded search region can contain many plausible candidates, while the highest raw similarity score may correspond to the wrong physical location.

PROPOSED RESOLUTION: The NOVA-Loc concept describes multi-scale handling, coarse search, Top-K candidate generation, structural and geometric verification, an ML-based verification component, confidence estimation, sub-pixel refinement, and a bounded recovery loop.
